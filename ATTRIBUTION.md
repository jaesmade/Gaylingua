# Vocabulary attribution

The imported vocabulary is adapted from **Wikipedia contributors, “Swardspeak”**:

- Article: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swardspeak
- Imported revision: https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Swardspeak&oldid=1376991241
- Contributor history: https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Swardspeak&action=history
- License: Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/

The imported dataset and its adaptations are available under **CC BY-SA 4.0**. This notice applies to the imported vocabulary, not to an assertion of ownership over individual words or an automatic license change to unrelated application code.

The snapshot includes all **83 vocabulary rows in the eight Conventions tables**, including the variants explicitly listed in those rows. HTML and citation markers were removed, variants were split into lookup aliases, concise display meanings were derived, context notes were added, and overlapping entries were merged with the original app vocabulary. Source labels, original word/concept text, and origin information are preserved in `data/wikipedia-swardspeak.json`. The lyric excerpt embedded in the Mag-Sharon derivation was omitted. Nursery-rhyme examples are parallel passages, not individual dictionary entries; their text was not imported.

The original 30 app entries and 12 phrase patterns were curated separately using Wikipedia and these research references. Original app sample sentences are not quotations from Wikipedia:

- “Swardspeak as a Communication Medium Among University Students: Empirical Evidence from the Philippines” (2023): https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369080094_Swardspeak_as_a_Communication_Medium_Among_University_Students_Empirical_Evidence_from_the_Philippines
- “How to Get Away with Saying Too Much: ‘Charot!’ as a Deflective Linguistic Armor in Everyday Filipino Talk”: https://sukisok.com/index.php/sjas/article/view/43

Source attribution is not linguistic validation. Usage varies across speakers, communities, regions, and generations. Some source entries and derivations are dated, vulgar, derogatory, or stereotyped; the app preserves them as documented vocabulary with context notes.

Run `npm run dictionary:import` to refresh from the article. Review snapshot changes and attribution before publishing a new revision. The app uses the checked-in dataset; it does not depend on Wikipedia being online during normal use.
